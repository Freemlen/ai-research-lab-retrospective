# AI Research Lab Retrospective

This repository documents a personal AI-assisted research experiment.

It does **not** claim a new result in gravitational physics.

I am not a physicist by training; I work with data. The project began as an attempt to see how far frontier AI systems could help one person explore a difficult technical field. Over time, the more interesting question became whether an AI-assisted workflow could be made skeptical enough to reject its own plausible-looking results.

## What happened

The project moved through several stages:

- numerical experiments in scalar-field gravitational collapse;
- adversarial diagnostic checks and controls;
- a novelty/relevance audit of the black-hole branch;
- a broader cross-domain search for better research questions;
- a theoretical active-matter branch that was ultimately stopped when its proof architecture failed to close.

Several apparently interesting results were downgraded or rejected by controls, prior-art checks, or later review.

The final conclusion was not a scientific breakthrough. It was a methodological lesson:

> **AI can generate research complexity faster than scientific value.**

## What survived

The public material here preserves only the compact, human-readable layer:

- `PROJECT_TIMELINE.md` — what was tried and where each branch ended;
- `SCIENTIFIC_STATUS.md` — what the project actually supports, and what it does not;
- `WHAT_WE_LEARNED.md` — lessons from the research process;
- `RESEARCH_GUARDRAILS.md` — rules I want to apply before any future large AI-assisted research project.

The full private archive contains much more evidence, code, raw numerical material, review packets and provenance records. It is deliberately **not** published here because volume is not the point of this repository.

## Important limitations

This repository should not be read as:

- evidence of a new black-hole mechanism;
- a proof about gravitational collapse;
- a validated new numerical method;
- an independent study of AI effectiveness in science;
- or a substitute for expert peer review.

Different AI contexts were useful for adversarial checking, but AI-on-AI review is not the same as independent human validation.

## Why publish this

The most useful outcome may be the process itself: a case where AI was repeatedly asked not just to generate results, but to attack them, reproduce them, compare them with prior art, and stop when the scientific value no longer justified the machinery.

A longer personal essay about the project is being published separately.
